## What a judge may attack

1. **“This is fake data.”** The business records are deliberately synthetic, but the sponsor processing is genuine. Show the five Nutrient request IDs and live source citations; do not use customer documents.
2. **“The coordinates were hand-authored.”** The portable fixture coordinates are deterministic test data. The separate live proof view contains Nutrient-produced citations and exact request IDs; the final video should still distinguish the live tab and ideally highlight at least the invoice amount and insurance expiry on-page.
3. **“Why not use an LLM?”** The release decision should not depend on probabilistic reasoning. Nutrient handles source-grounded extraction; deterministic rules own payment policy; uncertainty routes to a person.
4. **“Why is signature simulated?”** Safe-by-design judging should prefer a non-sending boundary until provider credentials and an explicit human approval are configured. If a sponsor requires a live send, use a test recipient and test document only after manual approval.
5. **“Is a hash chain production audit?”** No. It demonstrates tamper evidence, not durable compliance storage. A production design needs append-only external persistence, access control, key management, timestamps, and monitoring.
6. **“Can names be matched reliably?”** Current normalization handles punctuation and common legal suffixes. Production use needs vendor-master IDs and exception review; fuzzy name matching must never silently authorize payment.
7. **“Can users upload documents?”** Not in the fixture UI. That is intentional until size limits, MIME verification, malware scanning, retention, and authorization are implemented.
